KOPN 89.5 Jar Radio

A cheaper, single-station FM radio whose circuit board is the jar lid. It clamps onto a stock mason jar with a normal ring, has no firmware, charges from USB-C, and runs from USB alone or from an optional LiPo cell. This page is the full plan. The board is not generated yet. It waits for your answers to the open questions at the end.

RRevision: LiPo board, all parts on the top face

Status: plan for your review (no board generated yet)This replaces the "everything on the inside face" layout in sections 01–02 wherever they differ. The circuit, LiPo, charger, speaker, antenna network and jar stay the same. The 3×AAA variant is paused on branch aaa-battery.

What moves

  • Top (outside) face: every electronic part, placed by JLC in a single-sided Economic assembly. That covers the tuner, amp, charger, LDO, passives, the upright USB-C, both charge LEDs, and three JST sockets (battery, speaker, antenna).
  • Inside face: only the speaker (under the grille), the LiPo stacked on its back, the zip-tie slots, the CGW logo and text, and the solid ground ring under the band.
  • Plug-through holes: each lead comes up through its own hole beside its socket and plugs in on top. The battery and speaker leads come from inside the jar. The antenna wire can drop back inside through its hole, or run outside across the lid.
  • Volume/power pot: on top, fitted by JLC if a stocked switched pot exists (Q-T2), otherwise hand-soldered as before.
  • Whip: the plated M3 mount stays on top, on the same connection as the antenna socket.

Why it's better

  • One-sided assembly again: Economic PCBA (~$8 setup + 1 stencil) instead of two-sided Standard (~$25 + 2 stencils). That saves roughly $20–30 per order.
  • Ordinary top-side LEDs replace the reverse-mount parts. A red Basic part (C2286) and a green Basic/Extended part are about $0.01 each instead of $0.04–0.07, and it drops one or two Extended fees.
  • Right-angle sockets we already have numbers for: JST-PH S2B-PH-SM4-TB (C295747) and the XH G-Switch (C23067528). The vertical PH socket is no longer needed.
  • More room for parts. On top they only have to avoid the Ø36 grille, not the whole Ø49 speaker body, so the parts area grows by about 20%. Only through-holes (pot pins, plug holes, USB shell tabs, whip screw) must stay clear of the speaker and battery underneath.
  • Easy to probe and rework: every part is reachable without opening the jar.

01The jar is wide-mouth, so the board grows

The original Public Radio shipped on a wide-mouth 8 oz half-pint jar, not regular mouth. Its 70 mm board hung under a stamped stainless lid, inside the jar's roughly 76 mm opening, and a stock wide-mouth ring held the lid on. Retail copy confirms this ("fits any wide-mouth jar"), and so does the geometry: a 70 mm board can't pass a regular-mouth opening.

Here the board replaces the lid, so it takes the size of a wide-mouth flat lid (about 86 mm). That's good news. Inside the jar mouth the bottom side has a usable circle about 72 mm across, so the speaker, the battery and the parts JLCPCB solders all fit side by side.

02Board layout concept

Top is the outside face you see; bottom is the inside face, in the jar. JLCPCB assembles every SMT part on the bottom only, so this is a single-sided assembly order. On top go only the hand-soldered parts: the knob pot, the vertical USB-C, and the optional telescoping-antenna screw. The placement below is to scale but provisional. The final positions get refined during layout.

Top face (outside)

  • Volume/power knob: 9 mm pot with built-in switch, body on top, hidden under a 22–25 mm knob.
  • Vertical USB-C, hand-soldered. The cable plugs straight down into the lid.
  • Two charge LEDs (red = charging, green = full), mounted on the bottom and shining up through 1.8 × 2.4 mm holes (reverse-mount parts).
  • Optional M3 telescoping-antenna mount and a Ø3 mm hole for the wire antenna to exit.
  • Silkscreen: KOPN logo and name, "89.5 FM", and the grille pattern; white on black mask.

Bottom face (inside the jar)

  • All SMT parts, assembled by JLCPCB (single-sided order).
  • Speaker, Ø40 mm max, front pressed to the board under the grille, held by one zip tie across its magnet.
  • LiPo cell, 25 × 30 mm footprint, held by two zip ties, with a Kapton or foam pad under it so no part edges press on the cell.
  • JST-PH battery socket and JST-XH antenna socket. Different pitches, so the plugs can't be swapped.
  • The outer ring the jar rim and band clamp is solid ground copper on both layers, stitched with vias, with no traces or parts. No gasket for now.
  • CGW logo and name in silkscreen on this face.

Power: optional battery, charges while off

  • USB-C (power-only, 6-pin vertical). Each CC pin gets its own 5.1 kΩ to GND, which USB-C to C chargers need.
  • TP4056 runs straight from VBUS, ahead of the pot switch, so the battery charges whether the radio is on or off. A PROG resistor of 3.0 kΩ sets about 400 mA, which suits a 400–500 mAh cell (Q7).
  • Load sharing (Microchip AN1149): an SS34 Schottky from VBUS to VSYS and an AO3401A P-FET from VBAT to VSYS, gate pulled to VBUS with 100 kΩ to GND. With USB present, the radio runs from USB and the charger terminates correctly. With USB absent, the FET turns on and the battery runs the radio. With no battery fitted, the radio still runs from USB.
  • Pot switch sits between VSYS and VSW, so it powers only the radio and amp. When off, battery drain is a few µA (charger leakage plus the cell's own protection board).
  • Battery protection: use cells with a built-in protection board (PCM), which nearly every JST-lead LiPo has. No DW01A/8205A on the board (Q6).
  • XC6206P332 LDO feeds the Si4825 (its limit is 3.6 V; a LiPo reaches 4.2 V). The amp runs straight from VSW for more output.

Tuner: two resistors pick the station

  • Si4825-A10 (SOIC-16) is a dial-tuned chip with no MCU or I²C. It reads the ratio V(TUNE2)/V(TUNE1).
  • Station divider: TUNE1 → 86.6 kΩ → TUNE2 → 11.7 kΩ → GND, both 0.1%. On the assumed linear law this gives 89.4995 MHz (−0.5 kHz error; ±0.1% tolerance worst case is −4.9/+3.9 kHz).
  • Calibration safety net: an unpopulated 5 kΩ trimmer footprint in series with the bottom resistor, bypassed by a fitted 0 Ω jumper. If prototype 1 lands off-channel, cut the jumper, trim, measure, then pick final values.
  • Band select: a 500 kΩ divider from TUNE1 (432 kΩ over 66.5 kΩ, 1%) chooses band FM3: 87–108 MHz with 75 µs de-emphasis, the US setting.
  • TUNE1 decoupling is 100 nF + 47 µF placed at the pin, per AN738. RST uses 100 kΩ / 100 nF for power-on reset. VOL+ and VOL− are tied low (default maximum volume; the analog pot does the work). Crystal: 32.768 kHz with 22 pF caps.
  • FM input: ANT → 1 nF → FMI, with a ferrite bead of ≥2.5 kΩ at 100 MHz from FMI to RFGND for the DC path. AMI goes to ground through 100 nF.

Audio: NS8002 class AB amplifier

  • Why NS8002: about $0.03, an LM4871-compatible pinout, BTL output with no output caps, runs at 2–5.5 V, and it's class AB, so it puts no switching noise into the FM antenna. Class-D amps like the PAM8302 are cheaper per watt but can deafen a receiver with a wire antenna 2 cm away.
  • Signal path: AOUT → 1 µF → top of the B50K pot → wiper → 220 nF → 22 kΩ (Ri) → IN−; Rf = 220 kΩ, giving a gain of 20 (26 dB). That brings the ~80 mVrms tuner output to about 1.6 Vrms. Ri = 22 kΩ keeps the load on the tuner above its 10 kΩ minimum even at full volume (50 k ∥ 22 k ≈ 15 kΩ), and it bends the linear pot toward an audio taper. Adjust Rf on the prototype.
  • BYPASS gets 1 µF; VDD gets 10 µF + 100 nF at the pin. The shutdown pin is pulled to its active-run level, with an unpopulated jumper for the other polarity, because 8002 clones differ (Q11).
  • Expected output: about 0.45 W into 8 Ω on battery, about 0.8 W on USB.

04Antenna: wire included, telescopic optional

Default (ships in the box): a 75 cm wire antenna: 26 AWG stranded, silicone-insulated, crimped into a JST-XH 2-pin plug. It plugs into a locking XH header on the inside of the lid.

  • Why 75 cm: a quarter wave at 89.5 MHz is 83.7 cm; insulation slows the wave about 5%, which gives about 79 cm. Take off a few cm for the board trace and connector and use 75 cm.
  • Inside the jar (default): spiral it loosely around the inside of the glass, as vertical and spread out as possible, away from the speaker leads.
  • Outside: fish the free end out through the Ø3 mm hole in the lid and let it trail or hang. The plug stays inside, so nothing comes apart.
  • XH pin 2 is GND. Leave it empty normally. A second wire there can act as a counterpoise (half a dipole) for a fringe location.
  • Why JST-XH: it latches, pre-crimped pigtails are about $0.05, and its 2.5 mm pitch differs from the battery's 2.0 mm JST-PH, so the antenna can't be plugged into the battery socket by mistake.

Alternative: the plated M3 hole on top, on the same connection, takes a screw-base telescoping whip (about 7 sections, 15–19 cm collapsed, ~75 cm extended). Use one or the other, not both.

Will the coiled internal wire be enough?

For most of Columbia, yes. KOPN runs 36 kW from a site east-northeast of town, about 9–20 km from most of the city. That should give roughly 70–85 dBµV/m indoors.

Volume taperRV097NS pots with a switch are almost always B (linear). That's fine: the amp's 10 kΩ input resistor loads the wiper, which bends a linear 50 k pot into a usable audio-style curve (the classic "law-faking" trick). No extra parts needed.

07What gets delivered to the repo

KiCad project, JLCPCB-ready

  • kicad/jar-radio.kicad_pro / .kicad_sch / .kicad_pcb. Saved in KiCad 7 format, which is the version I can run here; KiCad 8 and 9 open and upgrade it.
  • Every symbol carries LCSC and MPN fields, so JLC's KiCad plugin and the BOM export just work.
  • kicad/lib/: project symbols and footprints (antenna mount, zip-tie slots, grille, pot, USB-C).
  • kicad/scripts/: the Python that generates and checks the design, so it can be regenerated with different station resistors.
  • kicad/jlcpcb/: gerbers zip, drill files, BOM.csv (Comment, Designator, Footprint, LCSC) and CPL.csv (bottom side).
  • Renders of the top and bottom, a DRC report, and the netlist check.

One board, any station

Only R_TUNE_H and R_TUNE_L change per station, plus the silkscreen text. The scripts take a frequency and print the resistor pair, so the same design makes a WNYC jar or a KBIA jar. The old EAGLE files stay in EAGLE_CAD/ and ARCHIVE/ for attribution.

LicenseNew hardware files: CERN-OHL-S 2.0, a reciprocal open-hardware license written for PCBs. The upstream LICENSE (GPL-2.0) and README (CC BY 4.0) stay in the repo with the credits to Spencer Wright and Zach Dunham.

08Build and test plan

  1. Answer the open questions: jar measurements first.
  2. Breadboard the Si4825 (optional but cheap): a SOIC breakout with a 100 kΩ pot in place of the station resistors. Find the real TUNE ratio for 89.5 MHz and check it against 0.1190. This removes the biggest unknown before spending on boards.
  3. Generate rev A: schematic, layout, autoroute, DRC, JLC outputs. You review the renders and BOM before ordering.
  4. Order 5–10 assembled boards (bottom side) and hand-fit the pot, USB-C, speaker and battery lead.
  5. Bring-up: USB only, then battery only, then charging with the radio off and on; check the charger terminates while playing; check the off-current.
  6. Reception walk-test: coiled wire vs trailing wire in a basement, downtown, and on the west side.
  7. Fit test: ring engagement with the 1.6 mm board and rattle with the speaker running; add a gasket or move to 1.2 mm only if needed.
  8. Rev B: lock in the station resistors, Rf gain, speaker and LED choice; then a small batch.
If you plan to sell itAn FM receiver is an "unintentional radiator" under FCC Part 15 Subpart B. Selling it in the US needs Class B emissions verification and a Supplier's Declaration of Conformity. A pre-scan at a local lab is a few hundred dollars. Also get KOPN's OK before putting their name or logo on the lid.
