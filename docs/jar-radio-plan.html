KOPN 89.5 Jar Radio

A single-station FM radio whose circuit board is the lid of a wide-mouth mason jar. It has no firmware, charges from USB-C, runs from USB alone or an optional LiPo, and has a wire or whip antenna. Board rev A is generated: schematic, layout, routing, and JLCPCB files. It is a first prototype. A few dimensions are still assumptions until the jar and parts are measured, and those are called out below.

Current build: wide-mouth 16 oz jar, Ø84 mm board, one 18650 in a holder hanging into the jar, Dragonstate HDK-4008BB-2 speaker, and no JST connectors. The 3×AAA variant is paused on branch aaa-battery.

What goes where

  • Top (outside) face, placed by JLC: every SMT part (tuner, amp, charger, protection, LDO, passives, LEDs), the upright USB-C and the slide power switch SW1. This is a single-sided Economic assembly.
  • Top face, hand-soldered: the volume pot RV1 (Bourns PTV09A-4, from Digi-Key: 3 pins + 2 mounting lugs).
  • Inside face, hand-fitted: the speaker, hot-glued under the grille, and the 18650 holder standing on end, hot-glued, with its + foot through a plated slot.
  • Wires, hand-soldered from the top: 2 speaker wires, the holder's − jumper wire, and the antenna wire (strain-relieved by a zip tie through 2 holes). The plated M3 whip hole stays as an option.
  • Holder 180° from the speaker: the speaker sits off-centre on one side. The holder slot sits on the opposite side of the centre, on the same line, so the two never collide inside the jar.

Why it's better

  • One-sided assembly: Economic PCBA (~$8 setup + 1 stencil) instead of two-sided Standard (~$25 + 2 stencils). That saves roughly $20–30 per order.
  • No connectors at all: no JST sockets, plugs or plug holes. That drops 2–3 Extended fees and ~$0.10–0.20 per radio, and nothing can be plugged into the wrong place.
  • Ordinary top-side LEDs instead of reverse-mount parts: red KT-0603R (C2286, Basic).
  • Easy to probe and rework: every part is reachable without opening the jar.
  • Hand work per radio: about 13 joints (pot 5, speaker 2 + 2 at the speaker, holder foot 1, − jumper 2, antenna 1) plus 2 hot-glue blobs and 1 zip tie.

The two parts you asked about

Speaker: FUET FS40MS0810-H5.0 (C482313)

  • A 40 mm, 5 mm-thin "micro" speaker, 8 Ω, 1 W, rated 92 dB, resonance 600 Hz, about $0.55 at 200+.
  • Sound: fine for talk radio and voices. Music will sound thin, because almost nothing below roughly 500–600 Hz. The GSPK4935 (45 mm driver in its own box, 95 dB) should have a little more body and be louder. Neither will thump. The sealed jar behind the thin speaker helps a bit.
  • Power: 1 W rated, so it's OK on battery (≈0.6 W). On USB the amp can reach ≈1.2 W, so I'd lower the amp gain slightly (R8 220k → 180k) so full volume doesn't overdrive it.
  • Big plus, space: at 5 mm thick it frees the inside of the jar. The LiPo could even lie flat beside it.
  • Need: its datasheet (or a photo) to see how it connects: solder tabs, bare wires, or a plug. That decides the socket.
  • Suggestion: buy 2–3 of each and listen in a jar before choosing. They're under a dollar.

Pot: Alps RK097111080J (C97444) won't work as-is

  • It's a 9 mm Alps RK097 through-hole pot (not SMD), 10 kΩ, and from the sibling part numbers it appears to be the horizontal version (shaft parallel to the board) without a switch. I couldn't open its datasheet from here, so please confirm.
  • No switch: it can't turn the radio on and off.
  • Horizontal: the shaft would point sideways across the lid, not up.
  • 10 kΩ: too low a load for the tuner output (minimum 10 kΩ, and the amp input loads it further). It would work with an extra 10 kΩ series resistor and a little more amp gain.
  • What to look for at JLC: a vertical pot with a switch, 20–100 kΩ. Search "potentiometer with switch vertical", "RK0971221" or "RK09D117" (Alps series with switch), "RV09 switch", "R0902N switch".
  • Fallback, fully JLC-placed: a vertical pot without switch (many stocked) plus a separate JLC-placed slide or push switch for power. That's 2 parts and a second control on the lid. Or keep the RV097NS and hand-solder it.

Speaker: Dragonstate HDK-4008BB-2 (C22371323)

  • A 40 mm, 8 Ω, 1.5 W, 93 dB driver with resonance 550 Hz. The listed response is 550 Hz – 4.5 kHz, which is telephone-band: voices are clear, music has neither bass nor sparkle. It's in the same quality class as the FUET FS40MS. The GSPK4935 is a little louder (95 dB), has its own back box, and is likely the fullest of the three.
  • "SMD" matters: it has solder pads, not leads with a plug. Either solder a short lead with an XH plug onto it (handwork), or, interestingly, have JLC solder it straight onto the top face. The grille holes then become back-vents into the jar (the jar is the speaker's box) and there are no wires at all. That needs its datasheet: height, pad layout, and whether it can survive reflow soldering.
  • Suggest: order 2–3 samples of each (≈$1 each) and compare in a jar before deciding.

Idea under review: one 18650 hanging into the jar

  • Electrically: yes. Same 3.7 V chemistry as the pouch, so the TP4056 charges it unchanged. At 2500–3500 mAh it runs about 35–50 h, against 5–7 h for the pouch.
  • Safety: good. Steel can, and no common disposable cell is 18650-sized, so nobody can charge an alkaline by mistake.
  • Protection: bare 18650s usually have no protection board, so add DW01A + FS8205A on the board (≈$0.08). Protected 18650s are 68–70 mm long and often don't fit standard holders.
  • Charge current: 500 mA (PROG 2.4 kΩ), about 6 h overnight. At 1 A the linear TP4056 would burn ≈1.3 W and run hot on this small board.
  • Mounting by one solder tab: no. A 45 g cell on a 77 mm holder is a lever arm. The joint will crack the first time the jar is knocked or the lid is set down hard.
  • Do this instead: use an 18650 holder with wire leads, plugged into the same PH battery socket like the pouch. Hold it with two zip ties through slots near its top end, or a small printed clip (you may print a cover anyway).
  • Space: a ~4 in (≈100 mm) tall jar with a 77 mm holder standing beside the speaker fits. It needs a ≈21 × 21 mm patch at the board edge, inside the Ø72 mouth circle.
  • Cost: cell ≈$1.50–3 (bulk, unprotected) plus holder ≈$0.20, about the same as the $2.83 pouch, for 6× the runtime and a replaceable cell.
Note on the jarA 4-inch-tall jar is not the 8 oz half-pint I assumed (≈2.5 in). Please send the jar's name or size and the lid/band measurements. The board diameter and keep-outs still depend on them.

Rev F.6: speaker datasheet, 18650 holder, protection chip comparison

Speaker: Dragonstate HDK-4008BB-2 (datasheet reviewed)

  • Ø40 × 5.3 mm, steel case, nylon cone with a cloth front, Nd magnet, 9.6 g. 8 Ω ±15%, 1.5 W rated (1.8 W for 1 min), 93 dB at 0.1 W / 0.1 m.
  • Response curve: about 94–98 dB flat from ~350 Hz to 2 kHz, a peak around 3 kHz, a dip near 4.5 kHz, falling below ~300 Hz. Better than "550 Hz–4.5 kHz" suggests: good for voice, OK for music, no bass.
  • Not reflow-solderable. The "SMD" label just means two solder pads on a small tab at the back, and it's rated for 60 °C operating / 70 °C storage. So: inside the jar, face against the board under the grille, held with hot glue on the steel rim (never on the cloth or cone). Two short wires from its pads go to the SPK+/SPK− holes (hand-soldered).
  • Amp gain 20 gives ≈0.6 W on battery and up to ≈1.2 W on USB, under its 1.5 W rating. Its 1.8 W limit is never reached.

18650 holder: BH-18650-B1BA002 (C2988620)

  • Holder stands on end, hanging into the jar. Its end tab goes through a plated oval slot and is soldered on top; the far terminal returns on a jumper wire to a round pad beside it. Both are in the footprint (slot provisional: I need the holder drawing for the tab size).
  • Hot glue: fine for assembly, but hot-melt softens around 60–80 °C, and a jar in a sunny window can get there. Use a high-temp glue stick, or neutral-cure silicone or epoxy for production. I'd also add two zip-tie slots so a tie wraps the holder's base as a backup.
  • Polarity: which end of the holder has the spring (normally −) and which has the tab? The footprint currently assumes tab = +, wire = −. The reverse-polarity FET makes a backwards cell safe either way, but the nets must match the holder.
  • Space (16 oz wide mouth): holder footprint ≈21 × 20 mm beside the Ø40 speaker inside the Ø72 circle, with plenty of depth.

Open questions for this revision

  1. Q-F1 · XB8089D datasheet. Please download it from the LCSC page for C79928. I need the pinout, the package (SOT-23-5 or ESOP-8?), the over-current trip and the over-discharge voltage. It's the last part of the protection stack I can't draw yet.
    It goes in the BAT− lead between J2 pad 2 and GND.
  2. Q-F2 · NTC: where does it sense? The TP4056 TEMP pin should see the cell's temperature, but the cell hangs ~80 mm down inside the jar. Your options:
    (a) a 0603 NTC on the board beside the slot, which only senses lid/air temperature;
    (b) a leaded 10k NTC glued to the holder and wired up, which adds 2 hand joints;
    (c) no NTC, with a fixed 10k resistor so TEMP always reads "OK".
    (a). It's free, JLC-placed, and catches the real risk here (charging in a hot car or a freezing garage). The XB8089D and the PTC still cover faults in the cell itself.
  3. Q-F3 · Charge current. PROG 2.4k gives 500 mA, about 6 h for a 3000 mAh cell from empty.
    500 mA. It's the safe default for USB-C with only 5.1k CC resistors, and the TP4056 stays cool on a sealed lid. 1 A (1.2k) would roughly halve the time but heat the lid.
  4. Q-F4 · Slide switch part. Please find a JLC-stocked SPDT slide switch, ideally Basic. Search MSK-12C02, SK-12D07 or slide switch SMD SPDT. The actuator direction matters:
    a side-slide PCM12-type has a lever that moves sideways, flat to the lid, about 1.5 mm high, so it's fiddly from above;
    a top-actuated SMD slide has a knob that sticks up and slides along the lid.
    Top-actuated if one is stocked. I'll redo the footprint for whatever you pick.
  5. Q-F5 · Pot final pick. PTV09A-4020F-A503 (no centre click, audio taper) or your 4220F-B503 (centre detent, linear)? Also, which knob? The shaft type (knurled or D) has to match it.
    4020F-A503, with a matching Bourns or generic knurled knob of about Ø15 mm.
  6. Q-F6 · Holder polarity. The plan has the holder's + end at the lid (through the slot), with the − end at the bottom returning on the jumper. OK, or flip it?
    Keep + at the lid. Either works electrically. With − on the jumper, a wire that pulls loose can only touch the GND-heavy board, never short the + rail to the clamp ring.
  7. Q-F7 · Orientation on the lid. Which way is "up" when the KOPN logo reads correctly? The sketch has the speaker on the left (west), the holder slot on the right (east), USB-C at the top (north) and the knob and switch at the bottom (south).
    As sketched. The knob and switch sit toward you, and the cable comes off the far side.
  8. Q-F8 · Green LED. Keep KT-0603G C12624 as a "charged" light? It's an Extended part, so ~$3 per order.
    Keep it. Without it, people can't tell when charging is finished.
  9. Q-F9 · ESD diode. PESD5V0F1BL (C5139344) is backordered. Please search ESD SOD-882 0.5pF or ESD 0402 5V low capacitance for a stocked part under 1 pF.
    If nothing turns up quickly, leave it DNP for the 5 prototypes. It only matters for static on the whip.
  10. Later, not blocking the prototype: Si4825 stock (only 96 at LCSC), so find a second source before ordering 100; an antenna wire source (26 AWG stranded silicone, cut to 75 cm); and an M3 telescopic whip if you want the option.

02Mechanical layout

Inside the jar

  • Speaker (Ø49 × 35 mm cavity) sits face-down under the grille, left of centre, and plugs into the PH socket marked SPEAKER. Point its mounting ear toward the antenna-exit side (12 o'clock): there's free board there, and anywhere else the ear would hit parts or the glass.
  • Battery lies flat on the back of the speaker with a Kapton/foam pad. Two 200 mm zip ties go down through the slots above and below the speaker, around the speaker and battery stack, and back up. That one strap set holds both.
  • The battery lead plugs straight up into the vertical PH socket. The antenna lead plugs into the XH socket; spiral it inside, or feed it out the 3 mm hole.
  • Parts stay inside Ø72 (jar mouth ≈ Ø76). Outside Ø73 is solid ground on both sides, solder-masked, with stitching vias and no tracks.

04Antenna

Wire (included)

  • One wire, 75 cm, on XH pin 1. Leave pin 2 (ground) empty. A second wire twisted alongside would partly cancel the signal. Pulled apart into a "T", two 75 cm wires make a dipole, but that's a fringe-area upgrade, not the default.
  • Prototypes: buy 2-pin XH single-end leads (longest offered, usually 50 cm), pull the black wire's pin out of the housing with a needle, and solder or crimp 25 cm more onto the red. XH leads up to 50 cm (eBay) · Tayda XH 30 cm.
  • Volume: order custom leads from an AliExpress harness shop. Search "custom JST XH 2.54 cable assembly" and ask for: XH 2-pin housing, 1 position populated, 750 mm 26 AWG stranded (silicone if offered), far end stripped and tinned. Typically $0.10–0.30 each at 100+.

Telescopic whip (optional)

  • Screws into the plated M3 hole on top, same connection as the wire. Use one or the other.
  • Buy M3 male thread, 7-section, ~75 cm extended: 5× M3 male 7-section AM/FM whips (eBay) · Silicon Chip 750 mm, 3 mm screw hole (female; needs an M3 screw from below) · uxcell 111 cm, 4 pc (longer than needed).
  • Expected reception in Columbia: the coiled internal wire is fine above ground; trailing wire or whip for basements and the far west side.

05Speaker and battery

Speaker: INGHAi GSPK4935PN-8R2W-L270 (LCSC C530575)

  • Ø49 × 35 mm enclosed cavity speaker with a Ø45 driver, 8 Ω, 2 W, 95 dB (loud for its size), PET cone, 270 mm leads on a 2.0 mm 2-pin plug, plus a mounting ear. $0.79 at 10+, $0.75 at 500+.
  • Being sealed, it sounds the same whatever the jar does. That's a good choice.
  • Plugs into J4, a vertical JST-PH socket that JLC places. It's the same part as the battery socket, so no extra fee. The speaker's 270 mm lead could reach either socket, so both are labelled (SPEAKER / BATT +/−) and placed far apart. A swapped plug won't damage anything instantly, but the radio won't work. Confirm its "2.0 mm 2-pin" plug really is PH-compatible when the speakers arrive.
  • The cost of using it: at Ø49 it takes most of the inside face, so parts are packed tightly into the crescent beside it. A generic Ø40 speaker (~$0.27) frees about 40% more room, if rev B needs it.

Battery: 500 mAh protected LiPo, PH2.0 (eBay, $2.83 each in 100s)

  • Size must be ≤ 30 × 35 mm (it sits on the Ø49 speaker back); 6 × 25 × 30 mm (602530) is ideal.
  • Check polarity on every batch before plugging in. The socket has + and − printed beside it.
  • Cheaper at volume: factory-direct 502530/602530 cells run ~$0.70–1.20 with a PH lead (e.g. Liter Energy 602530). Ask for the UN38.3 report; shipping needs it.
  • Runtime: ~5–8 h at moderate volume (~60–90 mA). Charge time ~1.5 h at 400 mA.

08Buy, solder, assemble

1 · Buy

  • JLCPCB: PCB + assembly: upload jar-radio-gerbers.zip, then BOM.csv + CPL.csv. Options: 2 layers, 1.6 mm, black mask, white silk, HASL lead-free, Standard PCBA, assemble both sides. The only top-side part is the USB-C, so you never solder it.
  • LCSC (same cart): GSPK4935 speaker C530575 ×1 per radio.
  • eBay/AliExpress: RV097NS B50K pot + knob kit (your $23/20), protected 500 mAh LiPo (your $283/100), XH leads, 200 mm zip ties, Kapton tape; optional M3 whips.
  • Local: wide-mouth 8 oz jars with bands (~$0.90).

2 · JLC assembles (inside face)

Everything except the volume pot: tuner, crystal, station and band resistors, reset detector, antenna parts, NS8002 amp, TP4056 charger, load-share FET and diode, LDO, both LEDs, the battery, speaker and antenna sockets, all passives, and the USB-C on the top face.

3 · You solder

  1. Volume pot: 5 pins + 2 tabs, through-hole, body on top.
  2. Calibration (one prototype): remove R2 + R3, fit a 100 kΩ trimmer in RV2 (1 = TUNE1, 2 = wiper, 3 = bottom), tune to KOPN, measure both halves, choose the final pair.

4 · Assemble

  1. Speaker face-down under the grille, ear toward the antenna-exit side.
  2. Pad + battery on the speaker back, two zip ties through the four slots.
  3. Plug in the speaker (SPEAKER), the battery (BATT, polarity checked) and the antenna lead.
  4. Knob on, lid on the jar, band on.

09Open questions and lookups

  1. Jar measurements: flat lid OD, band lip inner diameter, band inside height, jar mouth ID, inside depth, and how far the band turns on the stock lid.
    Board OD and keep-outs are one number each in gen_pcb.py; I'll rebuild once you send them.
  2. RV097NS pot, measured: pin pitch, distance from shaft centre to the 3-pin row and to the 2 switch pins, positions of the 2 support legs.
    The current footprint assumes 2.5 mm pitch, rows 6.5 mm either side of the shaft, legs at ±5 mm. Oversized holes absorb small errors, but please measure before ordering.
  3. Speaker ear: distance from speaker centre to the ear hole, and the hole size.
    Optional: I can add a screw hole so the ear bolts down as well as being zip-tied.
  4. Battery you bought: exact L × W × T and which wire colour is on which plug pin.
  5. Pot by JLC too? Search LCSC for RV097NS or "9mm potentiometer with switch". If one is stocked, JLC can fit it with its through-hole service and you'd solder nothing. Otherwise it stays the one hand-soldered part.
