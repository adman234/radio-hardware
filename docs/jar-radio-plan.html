KOPN 89.5 Jar Radio

A single-station FM radio whose circuit board is the lid of a wide-mouth 16 oz mason jar. It has no firmware: turn the knob and it plays KOPN 89.5. It charges from USB-C and runs from one 18650 cell hanging inside the jar. JLCPCB assembles every electronic part on the top face in one pass. You fit the speaker, battery holder and whip by hand.

  1. Upload the gerbers, BOM and CPL. In JLC's assembly preview, check each IC's pin-1 dot, the USB-C and the pot. Fix any 90°/180° rotation in the preview.
  2. Choose through-hole assembly for RV1 (the pot).

  4. Order 5 first. On those boards, check that KOPN comes in and that clockwise raises the volume.

02How it goes together

Hand assembly, per radio

  1. Holder Push the BH-18650-B1BA002's + foot up through the plated slot, solder it on top, and hot-glue the holder's end face inside the outline.
  2. − wire Run a short wire from the holder's far (−) foot up through the round pad above the slot, then solder it.
  3. Speaker Set the HDK-4008BB-2 on the top face, back down: magnet cup into the round cutout, potted terminal block into the slot between the fingers, its two exposed tabs on the triangular pads. From the inside face, heat each pad (about 300 °C, 2–3 s) and feed solder into the plated hole until it wets the tab. A dab of glue at the rim is optional. One speaker on its own, so polarity doesn't matter. Then put three small dabs of neutral-cure silicone (or hot glue) between the speaker rim and the board. That stops buzzing and takes strain off the solder joints.
  4. Whip Put the M3 stud through the WHIP hole, then add a washer and nut inside. A drop of thread-lock is optional.
  5. Knob Push a 6 mm D-shaft knob onto the pot.
  6. Cell Insert a flat-top 18650, + end toward the lid (the label inside says so). Charge it from USB-C until FULL lights.
  7. Jar Set the board on the jar mouth and screw the original band over it. The outer ring of the board is ground, clamped by the band.

Geometry

  • Board Ø84.0 mm (measured lid 84.3). Parts stay inside Ø72. Under the band (Ø74.3–84) both faces are solid ground with stitching vias, solder-masked.
  • Speaker Ø40 × 5.3 mm on the top face, centred 14 mm below the board centre. The board cutout is a Ø31 hole (magnet cup Ø16.2 × 3 mm, plus the back vents under the felt) with two fingers carrying the tab pads and a slot for the potted terminal block. The speaker rests on the ring between Ø31 and its Ø36.4 back face, and on the fingers.
  • Holder 77 mm long (86 mm over the feet), standing on end inside the jar from the slot, 180° from the speaker. Its 14.9 × 20.65 mm end face is glued between the slot and the speaker.
  • Knob The pot shaft rises about 29 mm above the lid. The R09512NS also has an M7 thread with an O-ring, which is unused.
  • Antenna wire hole (J3, 2 tie holes) stays on the board, unpopulated, as an alternative to the whip.
  • About 8 hand joints per radio, plus 2 glue blobs and a nut.

03Circuit

Signal path

  • Antenna Whip (ANT1) or wire (J3) → 1 nF C9 → Si4825 FMI, with 56 nH L1 to ground (AN738 whip network). The 0.5 pF ESD diode spot D4 is unpopulated.
  • Tuner Si4825-A10, 32.768 kHz crystal, band FM3 set by a 498 k ladder (330k + 100k + 68k). The RC reset is R1/C5; the brown-out reset spot U5 is unpopulated.
  • Station A resistor divider from TUNE1: R2 82k / R3 11k / R4 100 Ω, all 1% fee-free parts. Ratio 11.1/93.1 → 89.504 MHz on the assumed f ≈ 87 + 21 × ratio law. Worst-case 1% error is ±44 kHz (typically ±18 kHz), inside the ±50 kHz channel window. The first boards confirm the law; the RV2 pads take a calibration trimmer if needed.
  • Volume AOUT → 1 µF → R22 4.7k → 10k audio pot (keeps the Si4825 load ≥ 12.9k) → 100 nF → NS8002 (R7 47k, R8 680k: gain 29, BTL) → speaker.

Power path

  • USB-C (5.1k CC pull-downs, 5 V only) → TP4056 charger, 500 mA (PROG 2.4k). CHG = red, FULL = green. The temperature check is disabled by R21 0 Ω; R20/RT1 are an unpopulated upgrade.
  • Cell + → F1 (0 Ω now, PTC upgrade spot) → VBAT. Cell − → XB8089D (4.25 V / 2.5 V cut-offs, 10 A overcurrent, short-circuit, over-temperature, reversed cell) → ground.
  • Load sharing USB powers the radio through B5819W; the battery feeds it through AO3401A, which turns off when USB is present.
  • Switch The pot's rotary switch connects VSYS → VSW. The XC6206 3.3 V regulator feeds the tuner; the amp runs straight from VSW.
  • Charging still works with the knob off. Standby drain is a few µA.

06Open questions

  1. Knob. The R09512NS has a 6 mm D-flat ("FB") shaft, 15 mm long. Pick a knob style and colour. Is a shaft tip ~29 mm above the lid OK, or do you want a low, wide knob that hides most of it?
    A Ø20 mm aluminium or black knob, set-screw or D push-fit.
  2. Whip listing. Make sure it has an M3 male stud, not a female thread or M2.5. Collapsed length should be under about 20 cm so it stands neatly on a jar. Extended, aim for 60–80 cm (a quarter wave at 89.5 MHz is about 84 cm).
    Send me a listing and I'll check the thread and length.
  3. Cells. Which 18650 and from where? It must be flat-top and unprotected, and about 65 mm long, to fit the holder.
    Samsung 30Q, Molicel P26A or LG HG2 from a reputable US cell shop. Avoid unbranded "9900 mAh" cells.
  4. Stock for 50. The pot has 64 at JLC, the speaker 63 at LCSC, and the Si4825 was 96 when you last looked. All cover 50 today, but not by much.
    Order the 50-run parts soon after the prototypes pass, or pre-buy into your JLC parts library.
  5. First-board checks (no answer needed now): KOPN comes in clean (otherwise calibrate on RV2); clockwise raises the volume (otherwise I swap two pot pads); the holder foot fits the slot; the speaker sits flat with its tabs on the pads (the pads were drawn from your sketch, so check the fit on a real speaker); the board seats under the band.
  6. Protecting the cone. The speaker now faces out on top of the lid, so its Mylar cone is exposed. Do you want a cover later, for example a 3D-printed or laser-cut grille ring that sits on the speaker rim?
    Not for the prototypes. Listen first, then decide.

RRevision: LiPo board, all parts on the top face

Status: plan rev F.8 for your review (no board generated yet)This replaces the "everything on the inside face" layout in sections 01–02 wherever they differ. Current build: wide-mouth 16 oz jar, Ø84 mm board, one 18650 in a holder hanging into the jar, Dragonstate HDK-4008BB-2 speaker, and no JST connectors. The 3×AAA variant is paused on branch aaa-battery.

What goes where

  • Top (outside) face, placed by JLC: every SMT part (tuner, amp, charger, protection, LDO, passives, LEDs), the upright USB-C and the volume pot with built-in power switch (RV1, through-hole, fitted by JLC). There is no separate slide switch.
  • Inside face, hand-fitted: the speaker, hot-glued under the grille, and the 18650 holder standing on end, hot-glued, with its + foot through a plated slot.
  • Wires, hand-soldered from the top: 2 speaker wires, the holder's − jumper wire, and the antenna wire (strain-relieved by a zip tie through 2 holes). The plated M3 whip hole stays as an option.
  • Holder 180° from the speaker: the speaker sits off-centre on one side. The holder slot sits on the opposite side of the centre, on the same line, so the two never collide inside the jar.

Why it's better

  • One-sided assembly: Economic PCBA (~$8 setup + 1 stencil) instead of two-sided Standard (~$25 + 2 stencils). That saves roughly $20–30 per order.
  • No connectors at all: no JST sockets, plugs or plug holes. That drops 2–3 Extended fees and ~$0.10–0.20 per radio, and nothing can be plugged into the wrong place.
  • Ordinary top-side LEDs instead of reverse-mount parts: red KT-0603R (C2286, Basic).
  • Easy to probe and rework: every part is reachable without opening the jar.
  • Hand work per radio: about 8 joints (speaker 2 + 2 at the speaker, holder foot 1, − jumper 2, antenna 1) plus 2 hot-glue blobs and 1 zip tie.

The two parts you asked about

Speaker: FUET FS40MS0810-H5.0 (C482313)

  • A 40 mm, 5 mm-thin "micro" speaker, 8 Ω, 1 W, rated 92 dB, resonance 600 Hz, about $0.55 at 200+.
  • Sound: fine for talk radio and voices. Music will sound thin, because almost nothing below roughly 500–600 Hz. The GSPK4935 (45 mm driver in its own box, 95 dB) should have a little more body and be louder. Neither will thump. The sealed jar behind the thin speaker helps a bit.
  • Power: 1 W rated, so it's OK on battery (≈0.6 W). On USB the amp can reach ≈1.2 W, so I'd lower the amp gain slightly (R8 220k → 180k) so full volume doesn't overdrive it.
  • Big plus, space: at 5 mm thick it frees the inside of the jar. The LiPo could even lie flat beside it.
  • Need: its datasheet (or a photo) to see how it connects: solder tabs, bare wires, or a plug. That decides the socket.
  • Suggestion: buy 2–3 of each and listen in a jar before choosing. They're under a dollar.

Pot: Alps RK097111080J (C97444) won't work as-is

  • It's a 9 mm Alps RK097 through-hole pot (not SMD), 10 kΩ, and from the sibling part numbers it appears to be the horizontal version (shaft parallel to the board) without a switch. I couldn't open its datasheet from here, so please confirm.
  • No switch: it can't turn the radio on and off.
  • Horizontal: the shaft would point sideways across the lid, not up.
  • 10 kΩ: too low a load for the tuner output (minimum 10 kΩ, and the amp input loads it further). It would work with an extra 10 kΩ series resistor and a little more amp gain.
  • What to look for at JLC: a vertical pot with a switch, 20–100 kΩ. Search "potentiometer with switch vertical", "RK0971221" or "RK09D117" (Alps series with switch), "RV09 switch", "R0902N switch".
  • Fallback, fully JLC-placed: a vertical pot without switch (many stocked) plus a separate JLC-placed slide or push switch for power. That's 2 parts and a second control on the lid. Or keep the RV097NS and hand-solder it.

Idea under review: one 18650 hanging into the jar

  • Electrically: yes. Same 3.7 V chemistry as the pouch, so the TP4056 charges it unchanged. At 2500–3500 mAh it runs about 35–50 h, against 5–7 h for the pouch.
  • Safety: good. Steel can, and no common disposable cell is 18650-sized, so nobody can charge an alkaline by mistake.
  • Protection: bare 18650s usually have no protection board, so add DW01A + FS8205A on the board (≈$0.08). Protected 18650s are 68–70 mm long and often don't fit standard holders.
  • Charge current: 500 mA (PROG 2.4 kΩ), about 6 h overnight. At 1 A the linear TP4056 would burn ≈1.3 W and run hot on this small board.
  • Mounting by one solder tab: no. A 45 g cell on a 77 mm holder is a lever arm. The joint will crack the first time the jar is knocked or the lid is set down hard.
  • Do this instead: use an 18650 holder with wire leads, plugged into the same PH battery socket like the pouch. Hold it with two zip ties through slots near its top end, or a small printed clip (you may print a cover anyway).
  • Space: a ~4 in (≈100 mm) tall jar with a 77 mm holder standing beside the speaker fits. It needs a ≈21 × 21 mm patch at the board edge, inside the Ø72 mouth circle.
  • Cost: cell ≈$1.50–3 (bulk, unprotected) plus holder ≈$0.20, about the same as the $2.83 pouch, for 6× the runtime and a replaceable cell.
Note on the jarA 4-inch-tall jar is not the 8 oz half-pint I assumed (≈2.5 in). Please send the jar's name or size and the lid/band measurements. The board diameter and keep-outs still depend on them.

Speaker: Dragonstate HDK-4008BB-2 (datasheet reviewed)

  • Ø40 × 5.3 mm, steel case, nylon cone with a cloth front, Nd magnet, 9.6 g. 8 Ω ±15%, 1.5 W rated (1.8 W for 1 min), 93 dB at 0.1 W / 0.1 m.

18650 holder: BH-18650-B1BA002 (C2988620)

  • Holder stands on end, hanging into the jar. Its end tab goes through a plated oval slot and is soldered on top; the far terminal returns on a jumper wire to a round pad beside it. Both are in the footprint (slot provisional: I need the holder drawing for the tab size).
  • Hot glue: fine for assembly, but hot-melt softens around 60–80 °C, and a jar in a sunny window can get there. Use a high-temp glue stick, or neutral-cure silicone or epoxy for production. I'd also add two zip-tie slots so a tie wraps the holder's base as a backup.
  • Polarity: which end of the holder has the spring (normally −) and which has the tab? The footprint currently assumes tab = +, wire = −. The reverse-polarity FET makes a backwards cell safe either way, but the nets must match the holder.
