KOPN 89.5 Jar Radio

A single-station FM radio whose circuit board is the lid of a wide-mouth 16 oz mason jar. It has no firmware: turn the knob and it plays KOPN 89.5. It charges from USB-C and runs from one 18650 cell hanging inside the jar. JLCPCB assembles every electronic part on the top face in one pass. You fit the speaker, battery holder and whip by hand.

Before you click "order"

  1. Upload the gerbers, BOM and CPL. In JLC's assembly preview, check each IC's pin-1 dot, the USB-C and the pot. Fix any 90°/180° rotation in the preview.
  2. Choose through-hole assembly for RV1 (the pot).
  3. Board options: 2 layers, 1.6 mm, black mask, white silk, lead-free HASL, Ø84 round.
  4. Order 5 first. On those boards, check that KOPN comes in and that clockwise raises the volume.

02How it goes together

Hand assembly, per radio

  1. Holder Push the BH-18650-B1BA002's + foot up through the plated slot, solder it on top, and hot-glue the holder's end face inside the outline.
  2. − wire Run a short wire from the holder's far (−) foot up through the round pad above the slot, then solder it.
  3. Speaker Set the HDK-4008BB-2 on the top face, back down: magnet cup into the round cutout, potted terminal block into the slot between the pads, its two exposed tabs on the triangular pads. From the inside face, heat each pad (about 300 °C, 2–3 s) and feed solder into the plated hole until it wets the tab. A dab of glue at the rim is optional. One speaker on its own, so polarity doesn't matter. Then put three small dabs of neutral-cure silicone (or hot glue) between the speaker rim and the board. That stops buzzing and takes strain off the solder joints.
  4. Whip Put the M3 stud through the WHIP hole, then add a washer and nut inside. A drop of thread-lock is optional.
  5. Knob Push a 6 mm D-shaft knob onto the pot.
  6. Cell Insert a flat-top 18650, + end toward the lid (the label inside says so). Charge it from USB-C until FULL lights.
  7. Jar Set the board on the jar mouth and screw the original band over it. The outer ring of the board is ground, clamped by the band.

Geometry

  • Board Ø84.0 mm (measured lid 84.3). Parts stay inside Ø72. Under the band (Ø74.3–84) both faces are solid ground with stitching vias, solder-masked.
  • Speaker Ø40 × 5.3 mm on the top face, centred 14 mm below the board centre. The board cutout is a Ø16.8 hole for the Ø16.2 × 3 mm magnet cup (0.3 mm clearance per side), joined to a 10.4 mm wide slot for the potted terminal block. The two tab pads are mirror images 6.8 mm either side of centre, with 11.3 mm of board between them and 0.45 mm of bare board to the slot edge. The speaker's back face rests flat on the board.
  • Holder 77 mm long (86 mm over the feet), standing on end inside the jar from the slot, 180° from the speaker. Its 14.9 × 20.65 mm end face is glued between the slot and the speaker.
  • Knob The pot shaft rises about 29 mm above the lid. The R09512NS also has an M7 thread with an O-ring, which is unused.
  • Antenna wire hole (J3, 2 tie holes) stays on the board, unpopulated, as an alternative to the whip.
  • About 8 hand joints per radio, plus 2 glue blobs and a nut.

03Circuit

Signal path

  • Antenna Whip (ANT1) or wire (J3) → 1 nF C9 → Si4825 FMI, with 56 nH L1 to ground (AN738 whip network). The 0.5 pF ESD diode spot D4 is unpopulated.
  • Tuner Si4825-A10, 32.768 kHz crystal, band FM3 set by a 498 k ladder (330k + 100k + 68k). The RC reset is R1/C5; the brown-out reset spot U5 is unpopulated.
  • Station A resistor divider from TUNE1: R2 82k / R3 11k / R4 100 Ω, all 1% fee-free parts. Ratio 11.1/93.1 → 89.504 MHz on the assumed f ≈ 87 + 21 × ratio law. Worst-case 1% error is ±44 kHz (typically ±18 kHz), inside the ±50 kHz channel window. The first boards confirm the law; the RV2 pads take a calibration trimmer if needed.
  • Volume AOUT → 1 µF → R22 4.7k → 10k audio pot (keeps the Si4825 load ≥ 12.9k) → 100 nF → NS8002 (R7 47k, R8 680k: gain 29, BTL) → speaker.

Power path

  • USB-C (5.1k CC pull-downs, 5 V only) → TP4056 charger, 500 mA (PROG 2.4k). CHG = red, FULL = green. The temperature check is disabled by R21 0 Ω; R20/RT1 are an unpopulated upgrade.
  • Cell + → F1 (0 Ω now, PTC upgrade spot) → VBAT. Cell − → XB8089D (4.25 V / 2.5 V cut-offs, 10 A overcurrent, short-circuit, over-temperature, reversed cell) → ground.
  • Load sharing USB powers the radio through B5819W; the battery feeds it through AO3401A, which turns off when USB is present.
  • Switch The pot's rotary switch connects VSYS → VSW. The XC6206 3.3 V regulator feeds the tuner; the amp runs straight from VSW.
  • Charging still works with the knob off. Standby drain is a few µA.

06Open questions

  1. Knob. The R09512NS has a 6 mm D-flat ("FB") shaft, 15 mm long. Pick a knob style and colour. Is a shaft tip ~29 mm above the lid OK, or do you want a low, wide knob that hides most of it?
    A Ø20 mm aluminium or black knob, set-screw or D push-fit.
  2. Whip listing. Make sure it has an M3 male stud, not a female thread or M2.5. Collapsed length should be under about 20 cm so it stands neatly on a jar. Extended, aim for 60–80 cm (a quarter wave at 89.5 MHz is about 84 cm).
    Send me a listing and I'll check the thread and length.
  3. Cells. Which 18650 and from where? It must be flat-top and unprotected, and about 65 mm long, to fit the holder.
    Samsung 30Q, Molicel P26A or LG HG2 from a reputable US cell shop. Avoid unbranded "9900 mAh" cells.
  4. Stock for 50. The pot has 64 at JLC, the speaker 63 at LCSC, and the Si4825 was 96 when you last looked. All cover 50 today, but not by much.
    Order the 50-run parts soon after the prototypes pass, or pre-buy into your JLC parts library.
  5. First-board checks (no answer needed now): KOPN comes in clean (otherwise calibrate on RV2); clockwise raises the volume (otherwise I swap two pot pads); the holder foot fits the slot; the speaker sits flat with its tabs on the pads (the pads were drawn from your sketch, so check the fit on a real speaker); the board seats under the band.
  6. Protecting the cone. The speaker now faces out on top of the lid, so its Mylar cone is exposed. Do you want a cover later, for example a 3D-printed or laser-cut grille ring that sits on the speaker rim?
    Not for the prototypes. Listen first, then decide.
No other component questionsEvery assembled part has an LCSC number, a stocked part, and a footprint drawn from its datasheet. The DNP spots are documented in section 03.
